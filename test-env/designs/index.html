S4L Timeline — Design Review

Milestone badge: ten reveal patterns

All ten reuse the app's own tokens only — no new colors, same Courier title face, same card radius and shadow. What varies is the trigger (badge, link, tooltip, rail marker, per-stat chips) and the reveal (inline, popover, modal, drawer, sheet). #1 and #2 are the two you picked last round, kept as-is for reference.

Badge in the top-right pill row, same style as category/jurisdiction. Click it, a small card floats off using the card's own shadow.

No badge — a "Milestones" section under Sources, identical component and classes to "Witnesses Called" in WitnessCard.tsx. Each milestone is its own row.

A small circled-i sits right after the date, not in the badge row. Hover shows a compact tooltip on desktop; on touch, tapping "pins" it open (same target, no separate tap-vs-hover logic to build twice). Lightest-weight trigger of the ten.

No trigger to discover at all — a one-line summary sits under the subtitle, always visible ("2 milestones: 85 articles, 60 countries"). Click the line itself to expand the detail. Best if milestones should never be missed at a glance.

Reuses the exact "Read more" button already in TimelineCard.tsx — same class, same position below content, just a second line: "Show 2 milestones." Expands into plain rows, no box or border at all.

Badge opens a centered, dimmed dialog — plain header (event + date) and a stat list, no hero number this time. Good for treating a milestone as a small "moment" without the ceremony of the earlier gold version.

Same badge as #1, but it opens a right-side panel instead of a popover — more room per stat, keeps the event's date/title as a header for context while scrolling a longer list.

Same badge, but the panel slides up from the bottom edge instead of the side — the mobile-native pattern (drag handle included), worth it if most traffic here is touch.

Uses a piece of the app that none of the others touch: the vertical timeline rail. A dot with attached milestones gets a thin accent ring; clicking it opens a popover anchored to the rail itself, not the card. No card chrome changes at all.

Instead of one combined badge, each milestone gets its own small outline chip in the badge row ("85 articles", "$100K"). Click any one for just that stat's detail. Best when a reader might care about one specific number, not the full list.

Mockup only — not wired into the app yet.
